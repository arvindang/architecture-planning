# Project brief

Status: initial proposal derived from the supplied conversation. Working defaults below are recommendations, not additional user approvals.

## Core question

Can we go from a real parcel and a client's requirements to an intelligently designed, technically evaluated house, while automating useful repetitive work?

## Two connected outcomes

1. Develop architectural judgment by designing and comparing real spatial alternatives.
2. Build an orchestration layer that connects site evidence, design requirements, building geometry, automated checks, and review outputs.

The house is the test case. The software supports the design process; building a general CAD application is outside scope. Commercial validation, pricing, and a SaaS launch are separate future decisions.

## Proposed fictional client

A compact contemporary weekend home with natural light, a quiet workspace, and a strong relationship to the landscape.

| Attribute | Working brief |
| --- | --- |
| Occupants | Unspecified; establish household and guest patterns in programming |
| Bedrooms / bathrooms | 2 / 2 |
| Size | 1,400–1,600 sq ft; 1,500 sq ft working target |
| Construction | Timber construction proposed; framing system unresolved |
| Site | One real parcel in one jurisdiction; not selected |
| Budget | Unset; establish a ceiling before selecting a schematic design |
| Priorities | Daylight, bedroom privacy, workspace, garden access, tree preservation, simple construction |

Define the area measurement convention before comparing designs. Do not assume all room areas sum directly to gross building area. Natural ventilation is a design objective to investigate, not a demonstrated performance result.

## Scope

- Site dossier with provenance, preliminary constraints, and explicit missing information.
- Human-led programming and three initial spatial concepts; AI may critique the options.
- Parametric massing and a semantic building model as the design develops.
- Preliminary zoning geometry, solar studies, program checks, model-quality checks, and quantity estimates where inputs support them.
- A coherent schematic package generated from a shared model and traceable inputs.

## Boundaries

The six-week output is a schematic design and review package. It is not a construction set, permit application, engineering approval, or authorization to build. Detailed structural and foundation design, coordinated building systems, assembly details, specifications, and jurisdiction-specific permit documents are beyond this experiment.

No land acquisition, paid subscriptions, professional engagements, public launch, or external outreach is authorized by these planning documents.

## Success criteria

- One real parcel has an evidence-backed dossier that makes missing data visible.
- The prototype produces a repeatable report and envelope for supported, reviewed rules.
- The user creates and compares at least three spatial concepts, then develops one selected direction.
- Major design choices trace to a client requirement, site constraint, or recorded architectural intention.
- Drawings, views, and analysis outputs identify the model revision they describe.
- The final package exposes unresolved issues and identifies the questions to take to professionals.

An architect's critique would be useful validation, but securing that review is not assumed or already authorized.
